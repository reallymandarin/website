14 - 徐霞客——走遍中国的山河

Xu Xiake - Across China on Foot
493 字HSK 3

徐霞客，中国最早徒步的人，他400年前就走遍了中国大部分地方。他出生在一个有钱人的家庭，本来他可以读书，做大官，但他却选择了旅行。他从22岁开始旅行，用了差不多30年时间，几乎全靠双脚走遍中国的山河。他的旅行不是为了看风景，而是为了记录山、水和路。

徐霞客的旅行是有计划的，他从有名的山走到无名的山，从有人的地方走到没有人的地方，从大路走到小路，再走到没有路。他的旅行分为三个时期，一个比一个危险。开始是“看山”，去有名的山，就像旅游一样，走走看看；然后是“滨行”，他在大河和小河边上走，特别注意山和水的关系；最后是“进洞”，他进入山里的很深的洞，走到地下河。他经历过很多困难，比如，没有东西吃，迷路、受伤等等。

最后，徐霞客写了一本书《徐霞客游记》，这本书详细地记录了中国的“山、水、路”的情况：他写得很仔细，写山，记录位置，高度；写水，记录河是从哪里开始的，它分出了多少条小河；写路，他记录这条路通到哪里，在哪里断开了，哪一段难走……等等。

徐霞客的记录非常真实，400年前，他用行走理解世界、记录山河。他写的《徐霞客游记》到今天还在帮助人们了解中国。

英文翻译 · English

Xu Xiake was one of China’s earliest travelers on foot. Four hundred years ago, he traveled across much of China. He was born into a wealthy family. He could have studied and become a high-ranking official, but instead he chose to travel. Beginning at age 22, he spent nearly 30 years traveling, covering China’s mountains and rivers mostly on foot. He did not travel to see scenery; he traveled to record mountains, rivers, and roads.

Xu Xiake’s journeys were carefully planned. He walked from famous mountains to unnamed ones, from inhabited places to uninhabited places, from main roads to small paths, and then beyond the roads. His travels had three periods, each more dangerous than the last: first “mountain viewing,” visiting famous mountains like a tourist; then “riverside journeys,” following large and small rivers and paying special attention to the relationship between mountains and water; and finally “cave exploration,” entering deep caves and underground rivers. He went through many hardships, such as having no food, getting lost, and being injured.

At last, Xu Xiake wrote a book called The Travel Notes of Xu Xiake. It recorded China’s mountains, rivers, and roads in detail. For mountains, he noted their locations and heights; for rivers, where they began and how many smaller streams branched off; and for roads, where they led, where they ended, and which sections were difficult to travel.

Xu Xiake’s records were very accurate. Four hundred years ago, he used his travels to understand the world and record its mountains and rivers. His book still helps people learn about China today.
